Code GitHub Videos BibTeX

One arm stabilizes the object while the other acts. MFL learns their joint motion from demonstrations, revisits failures in simulation and uses video guidance to recover individual steps.

Abstract

Bimanual manipulation requires coordinated arm motion and object contact, but costly demonstrations cover only limited interaction states. We therefore propose MFL (More from Less), combining failure-region expansion in digital twins with deployment-time trajectory compensation for sparse-demonstration learning. A structured vision–language–action (VLA) policy first learns joint bimanual actions from real demonstrations using shared task context and training-time geometric supervision. To extend interaction experience, MFL decouples the background and manipulable objects into independent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) submodels and combines them with separate collision geometry and external physical parameters. Policy rollouts in this digital twin locate failures, around which feasible initial states are sampled for candidate generation. Complete trajectories that finish the task and pass physics and safety checks enter real-dominant retraining. The updated policy then identifies subsequent expansion regions. At deployment, 2D target trajectories extracted from asynchronously generated videos are aligned with the current state and mapped to actions. Valid references are fused with policy outputs only at high risk. Both base and fused actions pass through a shared quadratic-programming filter and nonlinear recheck. Across six real-robot task categories, the full system achieves a four-stage macro-average success rate of 89.00%, exceeding structured VLA by 13.17 percentage points. In a separate step-recovery evaluation, the complete compensation configuration achieves 67.78% recovery.

Method Overview

Sparse real demonstrations anchor policy learning. Accepted virtual trajectories extend training coverage, while valid references support local recovery at deployment.

Structured Bimanual Policy

The policy combines synchronized RGB from the head and both wrists with the instruction and joint state. It predicts 32 steps of absolute targets for both arms together. Each arm contributes five joint positions and one gripper value per step. During training, the predicted and demonstrated actions pass through the same kinematics and normalization.

Failure-Guided Digital-Twin Expansion

MFL reconstructs the background and movable objects as independent 3DGS assets from the real scene. Separate collision geometry and physical parameters from measurements, CAD/URDF or priors support interaction in Isaac Sim. Failed or high-risk rollout states identify where to sample feasible starting configurations. Only complete, successful rollouts that pass task, physics and safety checks join real-dominant retraining. The updated policy selects the next regions.

Step-Level Trajectory Compensation

Kling AI 3.0 generates a video asynchronously (about two minutes per request). The base policy continues to execute while the video is pending. When it returns, MFL tracks the target in the generated frames and aligns its 2D path to the current state and task stage. Head-camera depth, calibration and task-masked damped least-squares inverse kinematics map observable motion into targets for the ten arm joints. Both grippers inherit their policy commands.

Experiments

Six Bimanual Task Categories

Pouring, opening a microwave, opening a medicine box, wiping a board, a composite wiping–pouring task, and cooperative wiping in which one arm steadies the board.
