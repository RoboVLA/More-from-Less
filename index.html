More from Less: 3D Gaussian Splatting Digital Twins with Iterative Expansion and Trajectory Compensation for Sparse-Demonstration Bimanual Manipulation

Code GitHub Videos BibTeX

More from Less uses failures in simulation to choose where the two arms need more practice. During a recoverable step on the real robot, a video can supply a correction when risk is high and the reference is valid.

Real-robot demonstrations of the task interactions. Table 4 reports recovery on matched trigger segments.

Abstract

Bimanual manipulation requires both arms to coordinate their motion during contact. Collecting demonstrations is costly, so training data cover only a fraction of the interaction states the robot may encounter. We propose MFL (More from Less), which expands training data in a digital twin and compensates for local deviations during deployment. A vision–language–action (VLA) policy learns to predict both arms' actions from real demonstrations using a shared task context and geometric supervision. The twin represents the background and each manipulable object as separate 3D Gaussian splatting models and uses independently specified collision geometry and physical parameters. The policy starts candidate rollouts from feasible states near observed failures. Complete trajectories that achieve the task and pass physics and safety checks supplement real demonstrations, which retain the greater weight during retraining. The updated policy identifies where to sample next. During delay-tolerant recovery, a risk-threshold crossing, stage transition or local failure can trigger asynchronous video generation. A state-aligned 2D track from the video guides the policy action only when risk is high and the mapped joint reference is valid. All actions pass through the same safety filter. On six tabletop tasks designed for our SO101 bimanual robot, including pouring and board wiping, MFL achieved 89.00% four-stage macro-average success, 13.17 percentage points above the structured VLA alone. In a separate evaluation, full compensation recovered 67.78% of matched trigger segments.

Method Overview

Sparse real demonstrations anchor policy learning. Accepted virtual trajectories extend training coverage, while valid references support local recovery at deployment.

Structured Bimanual Policy

The policy combines synchronized RGB from the head and both wrists with the instruction and joint state. It predicts 32 steps of absolute targets for both arms together. Each arm contributes five joint positions and one gripper value per step. During training, the predicted and demonstrated actions pass through the same kinematics and normalization.

Failure-Guided Digital-Twin Expansion

MFL reconstructs the background and movable objects as independent 3DGS assets from the real scene. Separate collision geometry and physical parameters from measurements, CAD/URDF or priors support interaction in Isaac Sim. Failed or high-risk rollout states identify where to sample feasible starting configurations. Only complete, successful rollouts that pass task, physics and safety checks join real-dominant retraining. The updated policy selects the next regions.

Step-Level Trajectory Compensation

During recovery stages that allow delayed guidance, crossing a risk threshold, entering a new stage or detecting a local failure can trigger a request to Kling AI 3.0. Generation takes about two minutes. While it runs, the base policy continues through the shared safety filter. On return, the controller aligns the tracked 2D path with the current scene and stage. Head-camera depth, calibration and task-masked inverse kinematics turn observable motion into targets for the ten arm joints. The policy supplies both gripper commands.

Experiments

Six Bimanual Task Categories

We designed six tabletop tasks for our SO101 bimanual robot. Pouring, opening a microwave, opening a medicine box and wiping a board test different interactions with everyday objects. The sequential task combines wiping with pouring. The cooperative wiping task requires one arm to hold the board steady while the other wipes.

Evaluation and reproducibility notes

The ablation pool is evaluated separately from the stage and recovery comparisons. The repository provides the saved trajectory examples and documents the available records in the reproducibility notes.
